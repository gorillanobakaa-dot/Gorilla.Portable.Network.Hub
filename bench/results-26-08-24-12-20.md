# basic_rates on ath9k: measured

Answers open question 2 outright, and moves open question 1 forward without
closing it. All numbers taken on the VAIO SVE, Qualcomm Atheros AR9485,
channel 1, hostapd 2.10, 2026-08-24.

Scripts: `bench/forced-rate-run.sh`, `bench/multicast-data-rate.sh`.
Captures kept beside them.

## The result

**[measured]** The lever works, and it is worth 24x.

| hostapd config | basic rate set the beacon advertises | our AP's beacon TX rate |
|---|---|---|
| default `hw_mode=g` | 1(B) 2(B) 5.5(B) 11(B) | **1.0 Mbps** |
| `basic_rates=120 240` | 12(B) 24(B) | **12 Mbps** |
| `basic_rates=240` | 24(B) | **24 Mbps** |

Every row read out of a radiotap capture of our own AP's frames, filtered by
transmitter MAC `<our AP>`. The default row is exactly the legacy
b/g set `03-design-burst-carousel.md` predicted, and 1 Mbps is exactly the
number it feared.

## What this does to the carousel

**[inference]** Arithmetic on the measured figures, not itself measured.

The card does 5.36 MB/s of real unicast throughput as an AP (single hop, no
double-air penalty). Sending one payload to N laptops:

- **Unicast:** N x size / 5.36 MB/s. Cost grows with N.
- **Multicast:** size / (rate / 8). Cost is flat in N.

| multicast rate | effective | beats unicast from |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Mbps (default) | 0.125 MB/s | **never below 43 receivers** |
| 12 Mbps | 1.5 MB/s | about 4 receivers |
| 24 Mbps | 3.0 MB/s | about 2 receivers |

At the default rate the carousel is a worse idea than brute force for any
classroom under forty. With `basic_rates` raised it wins from the third
laptop onward. That is the whole design decided by one config line.

## What is still NOT established, and why

**The multicast DATA frame rate.** Beacons were measured. Group-addressed
data frames were not, because our AP transmitted none: with zero stations
associated there is nobody to send broadcast to, and the hostapd log for that
run shows `AP-ENABLED` followed by `AP-DISABLED` with no station event
between.

802.11 requires both beacons and group-addressed data to use a rate from the
BSS basic rate set, so they are expected to match. That is standard-derived,
**[inference]**, and this session did not measure it.

Closing it needs one associated client, which is the second device the notes
always said was required. It does not need a router: the client joins our AP.
A phone is enough.

### A wrong reading, recorded rather than quietly fixed

The first pass reported that multicast data frames stayed at 1 Mbps even with
`basic_rates=240`, which would have contradicted the standard and killed the
design. It was wrong. A monitor interface hears the **entire channel**, and
the filter `wlan.fc.type == 2 && wlan.da[0] & 1` was catching broadcast
frames from two neighbouring devices, `<neighbour A>` and
`<neighbour B>`. Filtering by our own transmitter MAC showed our AP had
sent no data frames at all.

Any monitor-mode measurement in a populated band must filter by transmitter
before it means anything.

## Method notes worth reusing

**Beacons are a free probe.** They are sent at the lowest basic rate, exactly
like multicast, and they are sent continuously with no client, no traffic
generator and no second machine. For questions about the basic rate set, sniff
your own beacons.

**Bound tcpdump by packet count, not by `timeout`.** `sudo -n timeout 8
tcpdump -w f.pcap` did not stop tcpdump. It ran for 40 minutes, produced a
64 MB capture, and then hung `tshark -V`. `-c 150` self-terminates.

**Do not guard the radio with a bash trap.** The first attempt did, the
harness killed the shell, the trap never ran, and the laptop was left with its
only network interface unmanaged and hostapd still running. The restore has to
be owned by something that outlives the process: these scripts arm a systemd
transient timer before touching the card, and disarm it only after the route
is back.
