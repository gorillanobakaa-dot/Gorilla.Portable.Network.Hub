# 0.9.10, in plain language

**The hub now explains itself, on the phones and on the teacher's screen, and
the wifi network no longer switches itself off when the room goes quiet.**

This release came out of a day of measuring a newer laptop's wifi, and of the
owner asking for one thing above all: that nobody, child or teacher, can be
left not knowing what to do next.

---

## On the children's phones

The class page was rewritten to be read by a child who has never seen it,
possibly in a second language.

- **Three numbered parts**, each with one line saying what its buttons do:
  1. *Files from your teacher* (READ or PLAY looks at it now, GET IT keeps a
     copy), 2. *Send your work to your teacher* (three numbered steps), 3.
     *Send a note to your teacher*.
- **Sending shows that it is happening.** The button counts up, *SENDING...
  40% - KEEP THIS PAGE OPEN*, so nobody taps SEND five times or closes the
  page halfway. Tapping SEND with nothing chosen says so straight away.
- **Every result says what happened and what to do next.** *Your work
  arrived. It is on your teacher's laptop now, waiting for your teacher to
  accept it.* A failure says that nothing was lost from the phone.
- **"Where do the files I GET go?"** A tap opens the answer for Android,
  Samsung and iPhone.
- **The sign-in window help only appears when it is needed.** Some phones open
  the page in a small "sign in to wifi" window where choosing files does
  nothing. The help for that used to tell every child they were in that
  window, which is not true in an ordinary browser. It is now folded under
  the question *Tapping Choose files does nothing?*
- The list of files is taller, and says it can be slid up and down.
- The name box no longer runs off the edge of a phone's screen.

The button names did not change: THAT'S ME, READ, GET IT, SEND IT TO YOUR
TEACHER. The guide and teachers already use them.

## On the teacher's screen

- **The first screen says what each choice does**, under the one you are on.
  Four short names cannot also say what each one is for.
- **The start screen explains every line** while you stand on it: the folder,
  the network's name, the password, and what happens when you press Start.
- **The file list says what the marks mean**: [x] handed out, [ ] not, [~]
  some of the files inside.
- **Press h while handing out** for one page that explains every key, what
  the class does step by step, and what to do if something goes wrong.
- The bottom line on that screen says what each key is for ("n message",
  "w work") instead of the program's own words for them.
- **The work-waiting screen** says that nothing is kept until you accept it,
  and that refusing moves work aside and never deletes it.
- The message after Stop no longer tells you to close the program: you can
  start again from the first screen.

## The wifi network

- **It no longer switches off during a quiet moment.** Windows switches its
  hotspot off after five minutes with no phone connected. The hub now tells
  Windows not to while it runs, and puts that setting back the way it was
  when it stops, even if the window is closed with the X.
- If Windows says the network is on but it never gets its address, the hub
  now switches it fully off and on again, which is what cured it by hand.

## How fast, on a newer laptop

Measured on a 2022 ThinkPad L15 with an Intel Wi-Fi 6 card, to a Wi-Fi 6
phone two metres away: **about 23 MB every second**, steady, which is three
and a half times what the 2012 laptop in the earlier results managed. The
laptop's processor stayed at about 4%. The full story, including what the
goal of 85% of the radio's top speed ran into, is in
[bench/RESULTS-WINDOWS.md](../bench/RESULTS-WINDOWS.md).

## Smaller things

- `hub serve ... --channel 13` froze without a word. It works again.
- Downloads a phone breaks off are now written in the hub's window, not only
  the ones that finish.
- `hub serve` on Windows no longer tells the class to open an address that
  belongs to a different network while its own is still starting.
- The help text (`hub --help`) no longer says Windows needs an administrator
  to make the wifi network. It does not.
- The note inside the Windows download (README.txt) was rewritten as a
  six-step start, with what to do when phones cannot see the network.

## New tools, for anyone who wants to check our numbers

`bench/transfer-watch-windows.py`, `bench/phone-speedtest.html`,
`bench/download-meter.py` and `bench/console-read.cs`, described in
[bench/README.md](../bench/README.md).

## Not changed

Linux behaves as before apart from the shared changes (the phone page, the
screens, the `--channel` fix). The Linux build of 0.9.9 and 0.9.10 has not yet
been run on a Linux laptop.
