# 0.10.0, in plain language

**The children and the teacher can talk to each other, and a child can ask a
trusted adult for help without anyone else knowing. The children's pages
come in six languages.**

This release came from the owner's first-hand knowledge of what happens to
children in some of the places this is for: some cannot say what is wrong in
front of their class, their family, or visiting officials. The old "send a
note" was one-way and hardly noticed. It is replaced.

> **Before this reaches a child:** the translations are first drafts that
> native speakers have not checked yet, and the private help design should be
> reviewed by child-protection professionals. Both are said in the guide too.

---

## Talking, both ways

- **Each child has a conversation with the teacher.** The child writes on the
  class page (*3. Talk to your teacher*); the teacher sees *NEW MESSAGE FROM
  THE CLASS. Press m*, opens the child, and types an answer. The answer
  appears on the child's page by itself within a few seconds.
- **The teacher can write first**, to any child on the network.
- **Seen marks both ways**: the child sees *seen by your teacher*; the
  teacher sees *(seen)*.
- The words never appear on the teacher's main screen, only a count, so a
  class looking over the teacher's shoulder reads nothing.
- Every ordinary message is kept in `messages.txt` in the received folder.

## Private help

Off until the teacher switches it on at the start of the lesson, choosing who
receives it:

- **the teacher**, on the laptop, or
- **a trusted adult**, such as a nurse or a protection officer, who signs in on
  their own phone. Sometimes the teacher is part of the problem.
- Optionally **a second adult** who may also sign in and read the record.

**For the child:** a HELP part on the class page opens a separate page, whose
title and address say nothing about help (a phone's history shows both).

- **I NEED TO TALK TO SOMEONE**: one tap, nothing to write.
- **Write privately**, if they want to.
- **HIDE THIS**: one tap back to the ordinary page, with no step to go back.
- **Nothing rings, buzzes or pops up.** A reply shows only as a small dot on
  the HELP button.
- **HELP is always written in English**, the word most children anywhere know,
  with their own language's word beside it.

**For the adult:** a list of the children who asked, marked *ASKED TO TALK*
when a child used the one tap. They answer, or **ask quietly to talk**: the
child sees *A trusted adult would like to talk to you. Is that all right?*
with YES, LATER and NO, and answers when it is safe.

**The record is locked.** Every private word, and which adult wrote each
answer, is kept in a file only the adults' passwords can open (`hub
private-record`). Guessing a password takes about a third of a second per
guess on a modern laptop. That keeps a borrowed or lost laptop from reading
it. It does not stop someone deleting the file, and it does not protect the
words while they cross the wifi: said on the page and in the guide.

## Six languages

English, French, Swahili, Portuguese, Dari and Pashto. The child chooses on the
first page and the phone remembers. Dari and Pashto read right to left, and
the page turns round with them. The teacher's screens stay in English.

## How it was checked

- 170 automatic tests, including the published test values for each piece of
  the lock, and a test that fails if any sentence on a child's page has no
  translation.
- A new tool drives a child's phone and the adult's phone end to end,
  13 checks. On its first run it found the adult's answers going nowhere: a
  real bug, fixed. All 13 pass, against a test server and against the hub
  running its real wifi network (from the same laptop, so not yet over the air).
- Not yet: a real phone in a real child's hands, native speakers, and a
  child-protection review.
