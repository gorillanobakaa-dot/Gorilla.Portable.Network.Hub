# 0.11.0, in plain language

**The hub is a window now, with buttons. A team leader opens a net with one
form and a briefing that everybody reads first. One room, like a radio net,
where everybody talks and a comms check counts who can hear.**

0.10.0 never shipped. Between it and this release the owner tested it with
real people and real phones, and three things were wrong enough to start
again: a teacher called the terminal program unusable; the messages were one
thread per child on the teacher's screen, with no room the whole class shares;
and the start screen's only way to the wifi and the chat was "hand out files",
which demanded a folder before the network would come up. A team leader
arriving in a disaster area, or a medical team at an outbreak, has no folder
and no class. They need the net open and one thing read by everybody who
joins. No relief team could have used the old front door.

---

## It is a window

Every screen the person in charge sees is a window with buttons, lists and
menus: the start screen, the folder picker, the settings, the net, who is on,
work sent in, the files, the join codes, the repairs. The terminal is still
there (`hub` in a terminal with `--tui`), but it is not the product any more.
The window opens full screen.

## Open a net

The start screen has two doors, side by side and the same size:

- **Open a net: you are in charge.** A form: your name on the net (*ZERO*,
  *Dr Mensah*, *Teacher*), the wifi name and password, and **the briefing**:
  the one thing everybody must know. Files are optional. One button brings
  the wifi up and opens the main channel, with your name and the briefing
  already in it. With no folder chosen, an empty folder *Gorilla Hub files
  for the team* is made next to the received folder; anything put in it later
  appears on the Files tab, where a tick hands it out.
- **Join a net nearby.** For a laptop that has just arrived: join the net's
  wifi, press Join, give a name, and the briefing comes first, then the whole
  conversation so far.

*Hand out a folder to a class* is still there, under *Other things this
laptop can do*: the same net, started from a folder of lessons.

## The briefing

Pinned above the channel on every screen. A phone, or a laptop joining,
gets it first with a big **I HAVE READ IT** button. The list of people on
the right says who has read it and who has not, and counts (*3 of 5 read the
briefing*). **Change briefing** rewrites it: everybody sees the new one at
once, it is said in the channel, and everybody's *read it* starts again. Not
having read the briefing shows ahead of an answered comms check, because a
comms check never ends on its own and new orders matter more than an old
call.

## One room, like a radio net

- **# main**: everybody on the net writes in it and reads it, as in IRC.
  You are *@* your name.
- **COMMS CHECK**: every device gets a big *I READ YOU* button; the list on
  the right counts the answers and shows who has not answered. Press it again
  to call only them.
- **Quiet the channel**: only you can write. HELP and private chats still
  work. **Mute** one person; **remove** a device (two presses).
- **Private chats** open as windows inside the main one: drag, make big, fold
  away, close. Several at once.
- **HELP** is one tap on every device, always on, nothing to set up: a red
  card at the top of your window with a sound, and the person goes red in the
  list. (0.10.0 put help behind a page and three settings; the owner's test
  showed that a cry for help does not survive a hoop.)
- A card with **Open** and **Later** when somebody writes to you.
- **A file into the channel** (2026-10-12). Next to SEND on every phone is a
  paperclip: a daily operations report, a sitrep, pictures, a zip, up to
  1 GB each, several at once. On a net the channel gets a blue line naming
  the file and its size with a **GET IT** button for every station; the
  laptop has it on *Sent to you* as before, and `messages.txt` says who sent
  what and where it went. In a class the same paperclip sends to the teacher
  only. The owner found the gap on 2026-10-12: a relief team member could
  type a few words but had no way to send the day's report, and an NGO
  works on written, typed reports for accountability.
- **The phone's wifi sign-in window, and gloves** (2026-10-12). When a phone
  joins the net, Android opens the page in its small *Sign in to network*
  window. Talking, HELP and the comms check work there; picking a file does
  not, because that window has no file chooser. The owner found this with
  the paperclip on a real phone. The page now knows when it is in that
  window and shows, at once, a card with three thumb-sized ways out: **OPEN
  THIS IN MY BROWSER** (hands this page's own address to the real browser,
  nothing to type), **or tap here** (a plain link) and the menu words
  (⋮ → *Open in browser* or *Use this network as is*). The paperclip there
  opens the same card instead of a dead picker. The hub already knows the
  phone by then, so the real browser lands straight in the channel, no
  second sign-in. On phones every button on the typing line is now at
  least 60 px tall, for wet gloves and numb fingers.
- **A net is a net, on the phone too** (2026-10-12). The name page said
  *Welcome to the class page* and *the name your teacher calls you*; the
  files list, the result boxes, the paused page and the old simple page
  said class, teacher and lesson. On a net every phone page now says net,
  channel, the person in charge (ZERO), in all six languages (the net's
  sentences are AI drafts like the rest). A test renders every phone page
  in net mode and fails on any word from the school yard.
- **Files in the channel, from the window too** (2026-10-12, the owner's
  first real-phone run of the paperclip). Under every file line the
  person in charge now has **Open** (where it waits) and **Keep** (into
  the received folder, off *Sent to you*); a laptop that joined the net
  has **GET IT**, which puts the file in its Downloads folder and then
  offers Open. And the same file sent twice (a retry, a second tap) is one
  file and one line: the phone is told *Already here*, the record notes
  the repeat. Before, one screenshot sent three times made three lines
  for one file, and the counts looked wrong.
- **A net accepts by itself** (2026-10-12). The waiting area and the Accept
  button are a classroom safeguard; on a net a report lands in
  *Documents\Gorilla Hub\received* the moment it arrives, the channel line
  says who sent it, and nothing waits for ZERO at 02:00. The owner: even
  knowing the hub it took a while to find the files in *waiting*; a ZERO
  under stress will not look. A class keeps the queue.
- **The hub knows what radio it is on** (2026-10-12). Windows called the
  radio plugged into the laptop "802.11n USB Wireless LAN Card"; it is an
  ALFA AWUS036NH, a long-range high-power 2.4 GHz adapter, and it carried
  the net all afternoon while the built-in Intel card was switched off. The
  status bar now says *net on ALFA AWUS036NH (Ralink RT3070), long range,
  2.4 GHz only*; the fix screen and `hub doctor` list every wifi radio by
  what it is, and which one carries the net. Read from the device's own
  ids (USB vendor/product, the MAC's first bytes), never from the driver's
  name; an unknown radio is reported unknown with its ids. Windows picks
  the hotspot's radio itself: to put the net on the long-range one, switch
  the built-in card off (the WifiRadios tool does it and undoes it).
- **One folder for the hub's own folders** (2026-10-12): received files go
  to *Documents\Gorilla Hub\received* and a net's files to
  *Documents\Gorilla Hub\files for the team*, instead of two folders loose
  in Documents. An older install's *Gorilla Hub received* keeps being used
  where it is until you move it in.
- Everything said in # main goes to `messages.txt`; comms checks, answers,
  who read the briefing and removed devices to `sign-ins.txt`, in the
  received folder.

The phones keep the web page (they cannot run a program); it is laid out the
same way, and folds the rooms and the people into a slide-out panel.

## How it was checked

- 183 automatic tests.
- `bench/net-chat-test.py`: a teacher and three phones in real browsers, each
  through its own relay so the hub sees separate devices: 39 checks, all pass.
- `bench/net-briefing-test.py`: the briefing on the real pages, set, read,
  counted, changed and unread again, and in the record: 20 checks, all pass.
- `bench/net-load-test.py`: 30 simulated phones, 3,570 of 3,570 lines
  delivered, no failures.
- Not yet: the window's new screens (start, Open a net, the briefing at the
  leader's end, the briefing-first screen on a joining laptop) have been built
  from an approved mock-up and compile, but have not been driven by eye on this
  laptop. Not yet over the air with real phones, and the owner's two-phone
  drops of 2026-09-25 were over the air: run `bench/hotspot-monitor.ps1`
  during the next phone test.
